However, a significant gap remains between current quantum hardware and the requirements to transcend what classical computers can achieve, with decoherence limiting the capacity of storing quantum information and the number of logical steps that can be executed reliably. In the long term, fault tolerance bridges this gap, but simply changing the numerical base from binary to higher base d is a viable way to 'squeeze more juice'---get more quantum information processing---from a quantum computer of given size.
